Pashmina Intelligence · Method note

How we calculate the numbers

Snapshot 27 Oct 2026 · India · for the dashboard at 01adityarathore-art.github.io/pashmina-intel-view

Every verdict compares a design or colour with its own normal: the same account's usual likes, the same store's usual sell-out rate, the category's usual search growth. A big brand or a viral post can't win on size alone, and small samples are pulled toward average before anything is judged.

L1 Search demand

Google reports close variants of a query with one shared number. Adding raw rows counts the same people many times (the raw total is a meaningless 3.9 million), so variants are first merged into demand pools: queries with the same 12-month pattern and the same words once spelling, plurals and synonyms (women = ladies = woman) are evened out. Only pools are added up.

Example. "pashmina shawl", "shawl pashmina", "pashmina woolen shawl", "pashma shawls" and 5 more all show 90,500 a month. They are one pool, counted once: 90,500, not 9 × 90,500.
  • Volume = average monthly searches over the last 12 months.
  • Growth = last 3 months ÷ the same 3 months a year earlier − 1. Three months smooth Google's rounding.
  • Removed as noise: a Class 6 English chapter called "The Kashmiri Shawl", the actress Pashmina Roshan, apartment projects named Pashmina, knitting patterns.

Result: 1.15M shawl searches a month; 421K are pashmina or Kashmiri (37%), up 85%, peaking in December.

L2 Search intents

Each pool goes into the first intent it matches, in this order, so every search is counted exactly once and the shares add up to 100%:

Is it real → Price → Where to buy → Brand → Design → Colour → Occasion & gift → Men → Women → Stoles & scarves → Learn & care → otherwise Just browsing

Example. "pure pashmina shawl price" mentions both purity and price; it lands in Is it real because that comes first. "Is it real" totals 27,460 a month = 6.5% of pashmina searches, +97% vs last year. Cost per click is what advertisers pay Google for that click, weighted by volume.

L3 Buyer pull: what buyers do

Each signal is a ratio where 1.00× means normal:

SignalHow it's worked out
Instagram likesEach post's likes ÷ that account's usual likes (median of its 3+ posts; otherwise the typical post or reel). Averaged on a log scale per account, then across accounts.
Sells outPieces of the design that sold out ÷ how many would have, at each store's own sell-out rate.
YouTube viewsEach video's views ÷ the typical video that names a design. Averaged per channel.
Amazon pullShare of "bought in past month" ÷ share of listings. Mass tier only, as Amazon is mostly imitation pashmina.

Then two safety steps:

  • Pulled toward average: adjusted = ratio ^ (n ÷ (n + k)). With few posts or pieces (small n) the ratio moves toward 1.00×.
  • Capped between 0.4× and 2.5×, so no single signal can decide alone.

Buyer pull = the geometric mean of the signals that have enough data.

Seller push

The design's share of Instagram posts and website listings that name a design (an average of the two). New launches = its share of pieces launched in the last 120 days and posts from the last 60 days ÷ its share before that.

Verdicts

VerdictRule
Stock nowBuyer pull ≥ 1.12× and seller push at or above the tier's median
Early betBuyer pull ≥ 1.12× but seller push below the median: wanted, few sell it
CrowdedBuyer pull ≤ 0.89× and push at or above the median (or launches growing ≥ 1.15×)
SkipBuyer pull ≤ 0.89× and little push
HoldEverything in between (within ±12% of normal)
Kalamkari, luxury tier → Stock now. 167 of 405 pieces sold out = 1.32× its stores' usual rate → adjusted for sample size 1.30×. Seller push is 13.5%, above the luxury median of 3.6%, so the verdict is Stock now (median price ₹1.75L).
Kani, all buyers → Crowded. Likes 0.96× (159 accounts) · YouTube 0.72× → 0.75× (32 channels) · sell-out 0.95× (2,055 pieces) → buyer pull = (0.96 × 0.75 × 0.95)1/3 = 0.88×. Seller push is 24.8%, far above the 7.3% median, so the verdict is Crowded, even though Kani is the most-searched design (13.8K a month).

Tiers come from real prices: Mass under ₹5,000, Mid ₹5,000–25,000, Luxury ₹25,000+. USD and EUR stores are converted at approximate rates (₹88, ₹101).

Trend Lab: what is trending now

Five signals, each against normal, adjusted and capped as above, then log-averaged into a trend score:

  1. Share of this month's posts (last 30 days) ÷ share in the 5 months before
  2. Likes vs each account's usual, this month
  3. Share of launches in the last 60 days ÷ share among older pieces at the same stores
  4. Google growth ÷ the category's growth: (1 + colour growth) ÷ (1 + 85%)
  5. Sell-out vs its store

Hot ≥ 1.25× · Rising ≥ 1.08× · Steady · Cooling ≤ 0.90× · fewer than 3 signals = Thin data. "Driven by" names only the signals pushing in the verdict's direction.

Maroon → Rising (1.15×). Post share 0.96× → 0.97 · likes 3.88× → 2.41 (11 posts) · launches 0.64× → 0.68 · Google +147% vs category +85% → 1.34 · sell-out 0.96× → 0.96 · score = (0.97 × 2.41 × 0.68 × 1.34 × 0.96)1/5 = 1.15×. Buyers love maroon on Instagram and search it more, but sellers are launching less of it: a gap.

The other tabs

  • Stock these: design + colour pairs that sell out more than their stores' other pieces at 2+ stores, ranked by that lift × the design's buyer pull. The price shown is the middle half of the pieces that actually sold out.
  • Opportunity matrix: the same sell-out lift for every design × colour pair. "White space" = 1.2×+ lift, 3+ sold, few listed. Example: Aari in navy: 7 of 35 sold out at 10 stores = 1.63×.
  • Launch radar: pieces with a publish date in the last 30 days (1,067).
  • Content playbook: 451 posts from 87 accounts with 3+ posts, each vs its own account's usual likes. Posts with a feature vs without it. Example: reels 1.60× vs photos.
  • Price ladder: 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentile of listed prices.

Limits

  • Likes come from posts that search engines show; most carry no price, so Luxury and Mid verdicts rest mainly on sell-outs.
  • "Sold out" can also mean discontinued; comparing within each store limits that. Big stores show only their newest 1,000 pieces.
  • Designs and colours are read from titles, captions and tags. A photo check matched well for Kalamkari, Sozni, Jamawar and Ikat.
  • Google rounds volumes into buckets, so small queries move in steps.
  • This is the first snapshot; week-on-week change starts with the next refresh (≈195 SerpApi searches; Google volumes reused for 25 days).
